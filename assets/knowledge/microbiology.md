# Microbiology

## Bacteriology — Gram-Positive Cocci
Staphylococcus: catalase-positive.
- S. aureus: coagulase+, protein A (binds IgG Fc), golden colonies; skin/soft tissue infections, abscesses, osteomyelitis, endocarditis (IVDU), pneumonia post-influenza, toxic shock (TSST-1 superantigen), scalded skin (exfoliatin), food poisoning (preformed heat-stable enterotoxin — rapid onset 2–6 h). MRSA: PBP2a (mecA gene).
- S. epidermidis: biofilms on prosthetic devices/catheters; novobiocin sensitive.
- S. saprophyticus: UTI in young sexually active women; novobiocin resistant.
Streptococcus: catalase-negative.
- Group A (S. pyogenes): β-hemolytic, bacitracin sensitive, M protein (anti-phagocytic); pharyngitis, impetigo, erysipelas, cellulitis, necrotizing fasciitis, scarlet fever (erythrogenic toxin), TSS-like; non-suppurative: rheumatic fever (type II hypersensitivity, molecular mimicry), post-streptococcal GN (type III). Anti-streptolysin O (ASO) titers.
- Group B (S. agalactiae): β-hemolytic, bacitracin resistant, CAMP test+, hippurate+; neonatal meningitis/sepsis/pneumonia; screen pregnant women at 35–37 weeks; intrapartum penicillin.
- S. pneumoniae: α-hemolytic, optochin sensitive, bile soluble, lancet-shaped diplococci, IgA protease, polysaccharide capsule (quellung reaction); MOPS — Meningitis, Otitis media, Pneumonia, Sinusitis. Most common cause of community-acquired pneumonia, meningitis (adults), otitis media, sinusitis. Asplenic risk.
- Viridans group (S. mutans, S. mitis): α-hemolytic, optochin resistant; dental caries (mutans), subacute endocarditis on damaged valves.
- Enterococcus (E. faecalis/faecium): γ or α-hemolytic; grows in bile + 6.5% NaCl; UTI, biliary infections, endocarditis; intrinsically resistant to many drugs; VRE.
- S. bovis (S. gallolyticus): endocarditis/bacteremia → screen for colon cancer.

## Bacteriology — Gram-Positive Rods
Bacillus: spore-forming, aerobic.
- B. anthracis: poly-D-glutamate capsule, edema toxin/lethal toxin; cutaneous (black eschar), inhalation (widened mediastinum, woolsorter's), GI. Bioterrorism.
- B. cereus: reheated rice — emetic (preformed toxin, 1–5 h) vs diarrheal (8–16 h).
Clostridium: spore-forming, anaerobic.
- C. tetani: tetanospasmin blocks GABA/glycine release (Renshaw cells) → spastic paralysis, trismus, risus sardonicus, opisthotonus.
- C. botulinum: blocks ACh release at NMJ → flaccid paralysis, descending; infant botulism (honey) — floppiness, constipation.
- C. perfringens: α-toxin (lecithinase) → gas gangrene (crepitus), double zone of hemolysis; food poisoning (late-onset).
- C. difficile: antibiotic-associated colitis (clindamycin, fluoroquinolones, cephalosporins); toxins A (enterotoxin) + B (cytotoxin); pseudomembranous colitis; treat oral vancomycin or fidaxomicin; fecal transplant for recurrent.
Corynebacterium diphtheriae: club-shaped, metachromatic granules, tellurite; diphtheria toxin (ADP-ribosylation of EF-2) → pseudomembranous pharyngitis, myocarditis, neuropathy; Elek test; toxoid vaccine.
Listeria monocytogenes: gram-positive rod, tumbling motility, intracellular (actin rockets), grows at 4°C (cold enrichment); unpasteurized milk/cheese, deli meats; meningitis in neonates/elderly/immunocompromised/pregnant; ampicillin (intrinsically cephalosporin-resistant).
Nocardia: weakly acid-fast, branching filaments, aerobic, soil; pulmonary (immunocompromised), brain abscesses; treat TMP-SMX. Actinomyces israelii: NOT acid-fast, anaerobic, branching; cervicofacial (lumpy jaw), sulfur granules, sinus tracts; treat penicillin.
Mycobacterium: acid-fast (mycolic acids).
- M. tuberculosis: cord factor, sulfatides; granulomas (caseating); Ghon complex; reactivation in apices; PPD/interferon-γ release assays; RIPE therapy; BCG vaccine.
- M. leprae: leprosy — tuberculoid (strong Th1, few lesions) vs lepromatous (Th2, many lesions, leonine facies, glove-stocking neuropathy); armadillos; dapsone + rifampin.
- M. avium complex: disseminated in AIDS (CD4 <50).

## Bacteriology — Gram-Negative Cocci and Coccobacilli
Neisseria: kidney-shaped diplococci, oxidase+, IgA protease.
- N. meningitidis: capsule, LOS endotoxin (Waterhouse-Friderichsen — adrenal hemorrhage), nasopharyngeal carriage; meningitis in teens/college (dorms), petechial rash; vaccine; rifampin/cipro prophylaxis for contacts.
- N. gonorrhoeae: no capsule, no vaccine; urethritis, PID, septic arthritis (young adults), ophthalmia neonatorum (silver nitrate/erythromycin at birth); antigenic variation of pili.
Haemophilus influenzae: requires factors X (hemin) + V (NAD) on chocolate agar; type b capsule (PRP) — epiglottitis, meningitis (unvaccinated); nontypeable — otitis, sinusitis, COPD exacerbations. Satelliting with S. aureus.
Bordetella pertussis: whooping cough; pertussis toxin (ADP-ribosylates Gi → ↑cAMP), tracheal cytotoxin; catarrhal → paroxysmal → convalescent; lymphocytosis; DTaP vaccine; macrolide treatment.
Brucella: undulant fever, unpasteurized dairy/animal contact; facultative intracellular.
Pasteurella multocida: dog/cat bite → cellulitis, osteomyelitis.
Francisella tularensis: rabbits, ticks; ulceroglandular; highly infectious.

## Bacteriology — Gram-Negative Rods (Enterics and Others)
Enterobacteriaceae: lactose fermenters (E. coli, Klebsiella, Enterobacter, Citrobacter, Serratia) vs non-lactose (Salmonella, Shigella, Proteus, Yersinia). MacConkey agar.
E. coli: #1 cause of UTI, gram-negative sepsis, neonatal meningitis (K1); strains — ETEC (traveler's diarrhea; heat-labile ↑cAMP, heat-stable ↑cGMP), EPEC (pediatric diarrhea), EHEC O157:H7 (Shiga-like toxin → bloody diarrhea, HUS; undercooked beef; no antibiotics), EIEC (dysentery-like), EAEC.
Klebsiella: currant-jelly sputum pneumonia (alcoholics, aspiration), lobar; large polysaccharide capsule; ESBL.
Proteus: urease+ → struvite staghorn calculi; swarming motility.
Salmonella: non-lactose, H2S+; typhoid fever (S. typhi — rose spots, relative bradycardia, carrier in gallbladder, Vi antigen), gastroenteritis (poultry/eggs), osteomyelitis in sickle cell.
Shigella: non-lactose, H2S−; very low infectious dose; dysentery (blood + mucus), invades M cells, Shiga toxin (HUS); day-care centers.
Yersinia enterocolitica: pseudoappendicitis (RLQ pain), daycare, pork/milk; grows cold. Y. pestis: plague (buboes), prairie dogs/fleas.
Pseudomonas aeruginosa: oxidase+, non-lactose, grape-like odor, blue-green pigment (pyocyanin); nosocomial, burn wounds, hot tub folliculitis, malignant otitis externa (diabetics), CF pneumonia, ecthyma gangrenosum; multidrug resistant.
Vibrio: comma-shaped, oxidase+, alkaline media.
- V. cholerae: rice-water diarrhea; cholera toxin (↑cAMP → Cl− secretion); no invasion.
- V. parahaemolyticus: seafood gastroenteritis. V. vulnificus: oysters, wound infections in liver disease.
Campylobacter jejuni: gull-wing, grows at 42°C, bloody diarrhea; Guillain-Barré, reactive arthritis; poultry.
Helicobacter pylori: urease+, curved; gastritis, peptic ulcers, gastric adenocarcinoma, MALT lymphoma; triple therapy (PPI + clarithromycin + amoxicillin/metronidazole).
Legionella pneumophila: air conditioning/water; atypical pneumonia + hyponatremia, diarrhea, confusion; silver stain, charcoal yeast extract; urine antigen.
Bacteroides fragilis: anaerobe below diaphragm; intra-abdominal abscesses; β-lactamase producing.
Bartonella henselae: cat-scratch disease, bacillary angiomatosis (HIV). Gardnerella vaginalis: bacterial vaginosis — clue cells, fishy odor with KOH (whiff test).

## Spirochetes, Mycoplasma, Chlamydia, Rickettsia
Spirochetes:
- Treponema pallidum: syphilis — primary (painless chancre), secondary (rash incl. palms/soles, condylomata lata, lymphadenopathy), latent, tertiary (gummas, aortitis, neurosyphilis — tabes dorsalis, Argyll Robertson pupils); congenital (saber shins, Hutchinson teeth, saddle nose, mulberry molars). Dark-field microscopy, RPR/VDRL (screening, cardiolipin) + FTA-ABS (confirmatory). Penicillin G; Jarisch-Herxheimer reaction.
- Borrelia burgdorferi: Lyme disease (Ixodes tick) — erythema migrans → Bell palsy, AV block, arthritis → encephalopathy. B. recurrentis: relapsing fever.
- Leptospira interrogans: animal urine, water sports; Weil disease (jaundice + renal failure).
Mycoplasma pneumoniae: no cell wall (no β-lactams), smallest free-living; walking pneumonia in young adults, cold agglutinins (IgM), Stevens-Johnson; Eaton agar; macrolides/doxycycline.
Chlamydia: obligate intracellular, biphasic (elementary body = infectious, reticulate body = replicating).
- C. trachomatis: serovars A–C (trachoma — blindness), D–K (urethritis, PID, neonatal conjunctivitis/pneumonia — staccato cough), L1–L3 (LGV).
- C. pneumoniae: atypical pneumonia. C. psittaci: birds.
Rickettsia: obligate intracellular, endothelial tropism; rash typically starts at extremities and spreads centrally (except typhus — trunk outward).
- R. rickettsii: Rocky Mountain spotted fever (Dermacentor tick; palms/soles involved; treat doxycycline).
- R. prowazekii: epidemic typhus (lice). Coxiella burnetii: Q fever (cattle/placenta, no rash, culture-negative endocarditis). Ehrlichia (monocytes — morulae, Lone Star tick) / Anaplasma (granulocytes, Ixodes).

## Virology
DNA viruses: Hepadna (HBV), Herpes, Adeno, Papilloma, Polyoma, Parvo, Pox. RNA: everything else.
Herpesviridae (all establish latency):
- HSV-1 (oral; temporal lobe encephalitis), HSV-2 (genital; neonatal), VZV (chickenpox → shingles; dermatomal), EBV (mono — atypical lymphocytes, heterophile Ab/Monospot; Burkitt, Hodgkin, nasopharyngeal, CNS lymphoma in HIV; oral hairy leukoplakia), CMV (owl-eye inclusions; retinitis/esophagitis/pneumonitis in AIDS, congenital — periventricular calcifications, hearing loss; mononucleosis-like heterophile-negative), HHV-6 (roseola — fever then rash), HHV-8 (Kaposi sarcoma).
Hepatitis viruses:
- HAV: fecal-oral, no chronic, IgM anti-HAV acute.
- HBV: blood/sexual/vertical; dsDNA (partially circular), reverse transcriptase; serology key — HBsAg active infection, anti-HBs immunity (vaccine = anti-HBs only), anti-HBc IgM acute window, HBeAg high replication, anti-HBe low; chronic → cirrhosis → HCC; vaccine available.
- HCV: blood; most likely to become chronic (~80%); no vaccine; treat DAAs.
- HDV: defective, needs HBsAg coat; superinfection severe.
- HEV: fecal-oral, high mortality in pregnancy.
Influenza: segmented RNA (antigenic shift = pandemics, drift = epidemics); hemagglutinin (attachment), neuraminidase (release); oseltamivir.
Rhinovirus: #1 common cold. RSV: bronchiolitis in infants; palivizumab prophylaxis. Parainfluenza: croup (seal-bark cough, steeple sign). Adenovirus: pharyngoconjunctival fever, hemorrhagic cystitis.
Measles: 3 C's (cough, coryza, conjunctivitis) + Koplik spots → maculopapular rash head-down; SSPE late. Mumps: parotitis, orchitis, aseptic meningitis. Rubella: mild rash, teratogenic (cataracts, deafness, PDA). Parvovirus B19: fifth disease (slapped cheek), aplastic crisis in hemolytic anemia, hydrops fetalis.
Rotavirus: #1 pediatric diarrhea (vaccine). Norovirus: cruise ship outbreaks.
HIV: CD4+ T cells, macrophages; gp120 (CD4 binding), gp41 (fusion); reverse transcriptase → integrase. Stages: acute (flu-like, high viremia) → chronic → AIDS (CD4 <200 or opportunistic infection). Opportunistic by CD4: <200 PCP pneumonia, <100 toxoplasmosis/cryptococcus, <50 MAC/CMV.
HPV: warts; 6/11 genital warts, 16/18 cervical/anal/oropharyngeal cancer; E6 (degrades p53), E7 (inhibits Rb); vaccine.
Rabies: bullet-shaped, Negri bodies; travels retrograde via peripheral nerves; bat/dog bite; hydrophobia; post-exposure vaccine + immunoglobulin; always fatal once symptomatic.
EBV/CMV congenital: CMV most common congenital viral infection. Zika: microcephaly.

## Mycology
Candida albicans: germ tubes at 37°C; thrush, esophagitis (HIV), vulvovaginitis, candidemia, endocarditis (IVDU); pseudohyphae.
Aspergillus fumigatus: septate hyphae, acute-angle (45°) branching; allergic bronchopulmonary aspergillosis, aspergilloma (fungus ball in cavity), invasive (neutropenic); aflatoxin → HCC.
Cryptococcus neoformans: encapsulated yeast (India ink, mucicarmine red); pigeon droppings; meningitis in AIDS; latex agglutination for capsular antigen.
Mucor/Rhizopus: broad, non-septate, right-angle branching; rhinocerebral in DKA/neutropenia (black eschar); amphotericin + debridement.
Histoplasma capsulatum: Ohio/Mississippi valleys, bat/bird droppings, caves; intracellular in macrophages; palatal/tongue ulcers, mediastinal granulomas.
Blastomyces dermatitidis: Great Lakes, broad-based budding yeast; skin/bone/lung.
Coccidioides immitis: Southwest US (San Joaquin Valley fever), spherules with endospores; erythema nodosum.
Paracoccidioides: Latin America, captain's wheel budding.
Dermatophytes (Trichophyton, Microsporum, Epidermophyton): tinea (ringworm); KOH prep. Sporothrix schenckii: rose gardener's disease, lymphocutaneous spread. Pneumocystis jirovecii: pneumonia in AIDS (CD4 <200); disc-shaped yeast on methenamine silver; treat TMP-SMX; prophylaxis at CD4 <200.

## Parasitology
Protozoa:
- Entamoeba histolytica: bloody diarrhea, flask-shaped ulcers, liver abscess (anchovy paste); cysts/trophozoites.
- Giardia lamblia: fatty foul-smelling diarrhea, bloating; campers; trophozoites (owl-face); treat metronidazole.
- Plasmodium: malaria — Anopheles mosquito; P. falciparum (severe, cerebral, ring forms, banana gametocytes), P. vivax/ovale (hypnozoites in liver → relapse; primaquine), P. malariae (nephrotic syndrome). Fever cycles.
- Toxoplasma gondii: cat feces, undercooked meat; ring-enhancing brain lesions in AIDS; congenital (hydrocephalus, chorioretinitis, intracranial calcifications); pyrimethamine + sulfadiazine.
- Trypanosoma cruzi: Chagas (reduviid bug) — dilated cardiomyopathy, megacolon/megaesophagus. T. brucei: African sleeping sickness (tsetse fly).
- Leishmania: sandfly; cutaneous (oriental sore) vs visceral (kala-azar — hepatosplenomegaly, pancytopenia).
- Trichomonas vaginalis: strawberry cervix, motile trophozoites; metronidazole (treat partner).
- Babesia microti: Ixodes tick, Maltese cross, hemolysis (asplenic severe).
Helminths:
- Nematodes (roundworms): Ascaris (Löffler, intestinal obstruction), Enterobius/pinworm (perianal pruritus, Scotch tape test), Trichuris/whipworm (rectal prolapse), hookworms Ancylostoma/Necator (iron deficiency anemia, ground itch), Strongyloides (autoinfection, hyperinfection in immunosuppressed), Trichinella (undercooked pork — periorbital edema, myositis, eosinophilia), Toxocara (visceral larva migrans).
- Cestodes (tapeworms): Taenia solium (pork; cysticercosis → seizures if eggs ingested), T. saginata (beef), Diphyllobothrium latum (fish; B12 deficiency), Echinococcus (hydatid cysts in liver/lung; dogs/sheep; anaphylaxis if ruptured).
- Trematodes (flukes): Schistosoma (snails; S. mansoni/japonicum — portal HTN, S. haematobium — bladder cancer, hematuria), Clonorchis sinensis (biliary, cholangiocarcinoma), Paragonimus (lung), Fasciola (liver).
Eosinophilia: invasive helminths (not protozoa).

## Immunology Essentials
Innate: barriers, neutrophils, macrophages, NK cells (kill cells lacking MHC I), complement, dendritic cells. Adaptive: T cells (MHC-restricted), B cells (antibodies).
MHC I: all nucleated cells → CD8+ T cells (viral/tumor). MHC II: APCs (dendritic, macrophage, B cell) → CD4+ T cells. HLA associations: B27 (ankylosing spondylitis, reactive arthritis, psoriatic, IBD-associated — "PAIR"), DR2 (MS, hay fever, SLE, Goodpasture), DR3 (SLE, Graves, Hashimoto, Addison, type 1 DM), DR4 (RA, type 1 DM), DQ2/DQ8 (celiac).
T helper subsets: Th1 (IFN-γ, macrophage activation, intracellular pathogens; IL-12 drives), Th2 (IL-4, IL-5, IL-13 — helminths, allergy, IgE class switching), Th17 (IL-17 — neutrophils, mucosal, fungi), Treg (IL-10, TGF-β — tolerance; FOXP3, IPEX).
Hypersensitivity:
- Type I (immediate): IgE, mast cells, histamine; anaphylaxis, asthma, allergic rhinitis.
- Type II (cytotoxic): IgG/IgM vs cell surface; hemolytic disease of newborn, Goodpasture, myasthenia, Graves, pemphigus, transfusion reactions.
- Type III (immune complex): antigen-Ab complexes; serum sickness, SLE, PSGN, PAN, Arthus reaction, farmer's lung.
- Type IV (delayed): T-cell mediated; TB skin test, contact dermatitis (poison ivy, nickel), type 1 DM β-cell destruction, MS, graft rejection (chronic).
Immunodeficiencies:
- B cell: X-linked (Bruton) agammaglobulinemia (BTK; recurrent bacterial/enteroviral after 6 months), selective IgA deficiency (most common; anaphylaxis to blood products), CVID (later onset), hyper-IgM (CD40L defect).
- T cell: DiGeorge (22q11 deletion — thymic aplasia, hypocalcemia, conotruncal heart defects; "CATCH-22"), SCID (IL-2Rγ X-linked most common; adenosine deaminase deficiency AR), ataxia-telangiectasia, Wiskott-Aldrich (eczema, thrombocytopenia, infections; WATER).
- Phagocyte: chronic granulomatous disease (NADPH oxidase defect; catalase+ organisms — S. aureus, Aspergillus, Serratia, Nocardia, Burkholderia; DHR test), leukocyte adhesion deficiency (delayed umbilical cord separation, no pus), Chédiak-Higashi (LYST; giant granules, albinism, neuropathy), Job syndrome (hyper-IgE; cold staph abscesses, eczema, retained primary teeth).
- Complement: C5–C9 deficiency → Neisseria; C1 esterase inhibitor deficiency → hereditary angioedema (no urticaria; treat C1-INH, icatibant; avoid ACEi); C3 deficiency → severe pyogenic infections; early classical (C1–C4) → SLE-like.
Vaccines: live attenuated (MMR, varicella, rotavirus, intranasal flu, yellow fever, BCG, oral polio — contraindicated in pregnancy/immunodeficiency) vs inactivated/subunit/toxoid (hep A/B, IPV, IM flu, tetanus/diphtheria toxoids, HPV, pneumococcal, meningococcal).

## Sterilization, Disinfection, and Lab Basics
Autoclave (steam under pressure, 121°C) kills spores — most reliable. Pasteurization, dry heat, filtration (heat-labile), ethylene oxide (plastics), glutaraldehyde (instruments), iodine/chlorine/alcohol (skin/surfaces; alcohol doesn't kill spores).
Culture media: blood agar (hemolysis), chocolate agar (Haemophilus, Neisseria — factors X/V), MacConkey (gram-negative, lactose), mannitol salt (S. aureus), EMB (E. coli green sheen), Löwenstein-Jensen (TB), charcoal yeast extract (Legionella), Eaton (Mycoplasma), Sabouraud (fungi), tellurite (C. diphtheriae), TCBS (Vibrio), Thayer-Martin (Neisseria selective).
Stains: Gram, acid-fast/Ziehl-Neelsen (mycobacteria), India ink (Cryptococcus), Giemsa (malaria, Babesia), PAS (Whipple, glycogen), silver (Pneumocystis, Legionella, fungi), mucicarmine (Cryptococcus capsule), Congo red (amyloid), Prussian blue (iron).
