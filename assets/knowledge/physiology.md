# Physiology

## Cell Physiology — Membrane Transport and Potentials
Resting membrane potential: ~-70 mV in neurons, determined mainly by K+ permeability (near K+ equilibrium potential ~-90 mV). Na+/K+ ATPase pumps 3 Na+ out, 2 K+ in (electrogenic).
- Nernst equation: E = (61/z) log([ion]out/[ion]in) at 37°C.
- Goldman equation accounts for multiple ion permeabilities.
Depolarization = membrane potential becomes less negative (Na+ influx). Hyperpolarization = more negative (K+ efflux or Cl- influx).
Transport: simple diffusion (O2, CO2, lipids), facilitated diffusion (GLUT transporters, ion channels), primary active transport (Na/K ATPase, H/K ATPase, Ca ATPase), secondary active transport (SGLT1 Na-glucose symport in gut/kidney, Na/Ca exchanger, Na/H exchanger).
Osmolarity vs tonicity: only non-penetrating solutes determine tonicity. Normal plasma osmolarity ~285–295 mOsm/L. Calculated: 2[Na] + glucose/18 + BUN/2.8 (mg/dL).

## Neurophysiology — Action Potentials and Synapses
Action potential phases: resting (-70 mV) → threshold (~-55 mV) → rapid depolarization (voltage-gated Na+ channels open) → overshoot (+30 mV) → repolarization (Na+ inactivation + K+ efflux) → hyperpolarization/undershoot → resting.
- Absolute refractory period: Na+ channels inactivated — no stimulus fires AP.
- Relative refractory period: stronger-than-normal stimulus needed.
Conduction: saltatory in myelinated fibers (nodes of Ranvier); velocity increases with diameter and myelination. Fiber types: Aα (proprioception, motor, fastest), Aβ (touch), Aδ (fast pain, cold), B (preganglionic autonomic), C (slow pain, warmth, postganglionic — slowest, unmyelinated).
Synaptic transmission: AP → Ca2+ influx at presynaptic terminal → vesicle fusion (SNARE proteins) → neurotransmitter release → postsynaptic receptors. EPSP (Na+ influx), IPSP (Cl- influx or K+ efflux). Temporal and spatial summation determine firing.
Neurotransmitters: glutamate (main excitatory), GABA (main inhibitory in brain), glycine (inhibitory in spinal cord — strychnine blocks it), acetylcholine, dopamine, serotonin, norepinephrine.
Stretch reflex (myotatic): monosynaptic — muscle spindle Ia afferent → α motor neuron (e.g., knee jerk). Golgi tendon organ (Ib afferent) senses tension → inhibits α motor neuron (inverse myotatic reflex, polysynaptic).
Autonomic nervous system: sympathetic (T1–L2, thoracolumbar, short postganglionic, NE at target except sweat glands = ACh), parasympathetic (CN III, VII, IX, X + S2–S4, craniosacral, long postganglionic, ACh). Both preganglionic neurons release ACh (nicotinic receptors). Adrenal medulla = modified sympathetic ganglion releasing epinephrine (80%)/norepinephrine (20%).

## Cardiovascular Physiology
Cardiac output = heart rate × stroke volume (~5 L/min at rest). Ejection fraction = SV/EDV (normal ≥55%).
Frank-Starling law: increased venous return → increased EDV (preload) → increased force of contraction → increased SV.
Pressure-volume loop: phases — mitral valve opens (EDV ~120 mL) → filling → mitral closes (S1) → isovolumetric contraction → aortic valve opens → ejection → aortic closes (S2, ESV ~50 mL) → isovolumetric relaxation → mitral opens.
- Increased preload: loop widens right (higher EDV, higher SV).
- Increased afterload: taller loop, higher ESV, lower SV.
- Increased contractility: wider leftward ejection, lower ESV, higher SV.
Heart sounds: S1 (AV valve closure), S2 (semilunar closure; A2 before P2 — physiologic splitting widens on inspiration), S3 (rapid ventricular filling — normal in young/pregnancy, pathological in HF = volume overload), S4 (atrial contraction into stiff ventricle — always pathological: LVH, ischemia).
Cardiac action potentials:
- Fast response (ventricular myocytes, Purkinje): phase 0 Na+ influx, phase 1 transient K+, phase 2 plateau (Ca2+ in via L-type, K+ out), phase 3 repolarization (K+ out), phase 4 resting.
- Slow response (SA/AV node): phase 0 Ca2+ influx (no fast Na+), phase 4 spontaneous depolarization (funny current If — Na+ inward) → automaticity. SA node fastest intrinsic rate (~100/min) → pacemaker; AV node ~40–60; Purkinje ~20–40.
ECG basics: P wave (atrial depolarization), PR interval (0.12–0.20 s; AV conduction), QRS (ventricular depolarization, <0.12 s), QT (ventricular depolarization + repolarization), T wave (ventricular repolarization).
Blood pressure regulation: baroreceptors (carotid sinus via CN IX, aortic arch via CN X) → NTS → short-term. RAAS (long-term): low renal perfusion → renin (JG cells) → angiotensin I → ACE (lung) → angiotensin II (vasoconstriction + aldosterone from zona glomerulosa + ADH + thirst). ANP/BNP oppose (vasodilation, natriuresis).
Microcirculation: Starling forces — filtration = Kf[(Pc - Pi) - σ(πc - πi)]. Edema causes: ↑capillary pressure (HF), ↓plasma oncotic (nephrotic, cirrhosis), ↑permeability (inflammation), lymphatic obstruction.
Coronary flow: maximal during diastole (especially left ventricle, compressed during systole).

## Respiratory Physiology
Ventilation: minute ventilation = tidal volume × respiratory rate (~6 L/min). Alveolar ventilation = (TV - dead space) × RR (~4.2 L/min). Dead space ~150 mL.
Alveolar gas equation: PAO2 = FiO2(Patm - PH2O) - PaCO2/R = 150 - PaCO2/0.8 (room air, sea level) ≈ 100 mmHg.
A-a gradient = PAO2 - PaO2; normal ~5–15 (increases with age). Elevated in V/Q mismatch, shunt, diffusion impairment; normal in hypoventilation/high altitude.
V/Q relationships: apex — high V/Q (ventilated more than perfused, high PAO2), base — low V/Q (perfusion > ventilation due to gravity). V/Q = 0 = shunt (perfusion without ventilation); V/Q = ∞ = dead space (ventilation without perfusion, e.g., PE).
Oxygen transport: O2 bound to hemoglobin (97%), dissolved (3%). Oxyhemoglobin dissociation curve sigmoid. P50 ~27 mmHg. Right shift (↓affinity, O2 unloading): ↑CO2, ↑H+, ↑temperature, ↑2,3-DPG (mnemonic "CADET face Right": CO2, Acid, DPG, Exercise, Temperature). Left shift (↑affinity): ↓CO2, ↓H+, ↓temp, ↓2,3-DPG, CO, metHb, fetal Hb.
CO2 transport: bicarbonate (70%, via carbonic anhydrase in RBC — chloride shift), carbaminohemoglobin (23%), dissolved (7%). Haldane effect: deoxygenated Hb carries more CO2. Bohr effect: ↑CO2/H+ shifts O2 curve right.
Compliance: C = ΔV/ΔP. Emphysema ↑ compliance (destroyed elastin); fibrosis ↓ compliance. Surfactant (type II pneumocytes, dipalmitoylphosphatidylcholine) ↓ surface tension, prevents alveolar collapse; deficiency → neonatal RDS.
Lung volumes: TLC = TV + IRV + ERV + RV. VC = TV + IRV + ERV. FRC = ERV + RV (cannot measure by spirometry — needs helium dilution/body plethysmography). Obstructive disease: ↑RV, ↑FRC, ↑TLC, ↓FEV1/FVC (<0.7). Restrictive: ↓all volumes, normal/↑ FEV1/FVC.
Control of breathing: medullary centers — dorsal respiratory group (inspiration, receives vagus/glossopharyngeal input), ventral respiratory group (forced inspiration/expiration). Pneumotaxic center (pons) limits inspiration. Central chemoreceptors (medulla) sense CSF H+ (from CO2) — main driver. Peripheral chemoreceptors (carotid/aortic bodies) sense ↓PaO2 (<60 mmHg), ↑CO2, ↓pH. In chronic CO2 retainers (COPD), hypoxic drive becomes important.

## Renal Physiology
GFR ~125 mL/min (~180 L/day filtered). Filtration barrier: fenestrated endothelium, basement membrane, podocyte foot processes — size and charge selective (blocks albumin).
GFR regulation: afferent arteriole dilation (prostaglandins) ↑GFR; efferent constriction (angiotensin II, low dose) ↑GFR. Autoregulation: myogenic + tubuloglomerular feedback (macula densa senses NaCl → adenosine/ATP constricts afferent).
Clearance: C = (U × V)/P. Inulin clearance = GFR (gold standard). Creatinine approximates GFR (slightly overestimates due to secretion). PAH clearance = renal plasma flow (freely filtered + secreted). Filtration fraction = GFR/RPF (~20%).
Tubular handling:
- PCT: reabsorbs ~65% Na+, water, all glucose/amino acids, HCO3- (via Na/H exchanger + carbonic anhydrase); secretes H+, organic acids/bases. Glomerulotubular balance.
- Thick ascending limb: Na-K-2Cl cotransporter (NKCC2 — loop diuretic target); impermeable to water → diluting segment. Generates medullary gradient.
- DCT: Na-Cl cotransporter (thiazide target); Ca2+ reabsorption (PTH-stimulated).
- Collecting duct: principal cells — Na+ reabsorption/K+ secretion via ENaC (aldosterone ↑, amiloride blocks); water via aquaporin-2 (ADH). α-intercalated cells secrete H+; β-intercalated secrete HCO3-.
Countercurrent multiplication: loop of Henle + vasa recta create medullary osmotic gradient (up to 1200 mOsm). Urea recycling contributes.
ADH (vasopressin): from posterior pituitary; released by ↑osmolarity (osmoreceptors) or ↓volume (baroreceptors); V2 receptors → aquaporin-2 insertion → concentrated urine. Diabetes insipidus: central (no ADH) vs nephrogenic (no response) → dilute polyuria.
RAAS in kidney: juxtaglomerular apparatus — macula densa (DCT), JG cells (renin), extraglomerular mesangium. Renin release: ↓renal perfusion pressure, ↓NaCl at macula densa, β1 sympathetic stimulation.
Acid-base in kidney: PCT reclaims filtered HCO3-; collecting duct generates new HCO3- via H+ secretion (buffered by phosphate and NH3/NH4+). K+ handling linked to H+: hypokalemia → intracellular H+ shift → metabolic alkalosis.

## Gastrointestinal Physiology
Saliva: hypotonic; α-amylase (ptyalin), lingual lipase; parasympathetic ↑ secretion (watery), sympathetic ↑ (mucous).
Gastric secretion:
- Parietal cells (body/fundus): HCl (H/K ATPase) + intrinsic factor. Stimulated by ACh (M3), gastrin (CCK-B), histamine (H2 — synergistic potentiation). Inhibited by somatostatin, prostaglandins, secretin.
- Chief cells: pepsinogen (activated by HCl → pepsin), gastric lipase.
- G cells (antrum): gastrin — stimulated by peptides, vagal GRP, stomach distension; inhibited by pH <1.5 (somatostatin).
- D cells: somatostatin. Mucous cells: bicarbonate/mucus barrier (prostaglandin-dependent — NSAIDs impair → ulcers).
Phases: cephalic (30%, vagal), gastric (60%, distension/peptides), intestinal (10%).
Pancreas: acinar — enzymes (trypsinogen activated by enterokinase/enteropeptidase in duodenum → trypsin activates others; trypsin inhibitor prevents autodigestion). Ductal — HCO3- rich fluid (secretin-stimulated). CCK stimulates enzyme secretion + gallbladder contraction.
Bile: bile salts (emulsification), bilirubin, cholesterol; enterohepatic circulation — 95% reabsorbed in terminal ileum. Gallstones if cholesterol supersaturation.
Small intestine: brush border enzymes (lactase, sucrase, maltase, peptidases). Absorption: iron (duodenum, Fe2+ via DMT1), folate (jejunum), B12 (terminal ileum + intrinsic factor). "Iron Fist Bro" — Iron duodenum, Folate jejunum, B12 ileum. Fat absorption: micelles → enterocytes → chylomicrons → lacteals.
Motility: migrating motor complex (MMC) between meals — motilin-driven "housekeeper". Peristalsis behind bolus (contraction) and ahead (relaxation) via ENS. Swallowing: primary peristalsis (vagus), secondary (local reflex). LES tone prevents reflux; VIP/NO relax it (achalasia = loss of inhibitory neurons).
Hormones: gastrin (G, antrum), CCK (I, duodenum/jejunum — gallbladder contraction, pancreatic enzymes, satiety), secretin (S, duodenum — pancreatic HCO3-, inhibits gastric acid), GIP (K — insulin release), motilin (MMC), somatostatin (D — universal inhibitor), ghrelin (stomach — hunger), leptin (adipose — satiety).

## Endocrine Physiology
Hypothalamic-pituitary axis: hypothalamic releasing/inhibiting hormones → anterior pituitary via portal system. Anterior pituitary (adenohypophysis): GH, prolactin, FSH, LH, TSH, ACTH (from POMC with MSH/endorphins). Posterior pituitary (neurohypophysis): ADH + oxytocin (made in hypothalamus, stored/released posteriorly).
Feedback: mostly negative (cortisol inhibits CRH/ACTH; T3/T4 inhibit TRH/TSH). Prolactin under tonic dopamine inhibition (dopamine = PIF); TRH stimulates prolactin.
Thyroid: TSH → T4 (main product) + T3; peripheral conversion T4→T3 by deiodinases (rT3 inactive). T3 more active. Bound to TBG (↑TBG in pregnancy/estrogen → ↑total T4, normal free T4).
Adrenal cortex zones (mnemonic GFR — "Salt, Sugar, Sex"): zona Glomerulosa (mineralocorticoids — aldosterone, RAAS-regulated), zona Fasciculata (glucocorticoids — cortisol, ACTH-regulated), zona Reticularis (androgens — DHEA). Medulla: catecholamines.
Cortisol effects: ↑gluconeogenesis, ↑insulin resistance, anti-inflammatory (inhibits PLA2 via lipocortin, ↓IL-2), permissive for catecholamines, ↑vascular tone. Diurnal rhythm (peak early morning).
Calcium homeostasis: PTH (↑Ca, ↓PO4 — bone resorption, renal Ca reabsorption, 1α-hydroxylase → calcitriol → gut absorption). Calcitonin (thyroid C cells — minor ↓Ca). Vitamin D: skin (7-dehydrocholesterol + UV) → liver 25-hydroxylation → kidney 1α-hydroxylation (PTH-stimulated) → 1,25-(OH)2D3 (calcitriol).
Insulin: β cells; released by ↑glucose (GLUT2 → ATP → K+ channel closure → depolarization → Ca2+ → release), amino acids, incretins (GIP/GLP-1), parasympathetic. Inhibited by α2 sympathetic, somatostatin. Anabolic: ↑glucose uptake (GLUT4 in muscle/adipose), glycogen/lipid/protein synthesis. Glucagon: α cells, catabolic, ↑gluconeogenesis/glycogenolysis.
GLUT transporters: GLUT1 (brain/RBC, insulin-independent), GLUT2 (liver, β cells, kidney, bidirectional), GLUT3 (neurons), GLUT4 (muscle/adipose, insulin-dependent), GLUT5 (fructose, gut).

## Reproductive Physiology
Male: GnRH (pulsatile) → LH (Leydig cells → testosterone), FSH (Sertoli cells → support spermatogenesis, inhibin B, androgen-binding protein). Testosterone negative feedback on LH; inhibin B on FSH. Sertoli cells form blood-testis barrier. Spermatogenesis ~64–72 days.
Female cycle: follicular phase — FSH recruits follicles; granulosa cells convert theca-derived androgens to estrogen (aromatase, "two-cell, two-gonadotropin" model). Estrogen peak → LH surge (positive feedback) → ovulation (day 14). Luteal phase — corpus luteum secretes progesterone (+ estrogen); progesterone ↑ basal body temperature. If no implantation → corpus luteum degenerates (day ~24) → ↓progesterone/estrogen → menses. hCG from trophoblast rescues corpus luteum.
Endometrium: proliferative phase (estrogen-driven), secretory phase (progesterone-driven — glycogen-rich glands), menstruation (spiral artery constriction from progesterone withdrawal).
Pregnancy: hCG detectable ~8–11 days post-conception. Placenta produces hCG (1st trimester), then progesterone/estrogen (hPL, relaxin). Estriol main estrogen of pregnancy.
Menopause: ↓estrogen, ↑FSH/LH (loss of negative feedback), ↓inhibin B earliest marker.

## Hematology Basics
Erythropoiesis: EPO (kidney peritubular cells, hypoxia-inducible factor) stimulates RBC production. Requires iron, B12, folate. RBC lifespan 120 days; removed by splenic macrophages. Heme → biliverdin → unconjugated bilirubin (albumin-bound) → liver conjugation (UDP-glucuronosyltransferase) → conjugated bilirubin → bile → gut bacteria → urobilinogen (some reabsorbed/excreted in urine) / stercobilin (feces color).
Hemoglobin: HbA (α2β2, adult), HbF (α2γ2, fetal — higher O2 affinity, resists 2,3-DPG), HbA2 (α2δ2). Cooperativity → sigmoid curve.
Coagulation: intrinsic (XII, XI, IX, VIII — measured by aPTT), extrinsic (tissue factor + VII — measured by PT/INR), common (X, V, II, I — fibrin). Thrombin converts fibrinogen → fibrin, activates XIII (crosslinks). Antithrombin inhibits thrombin/Xa (heparin potentiates). Protein C/S inactivate Va/VIIIa. Warfarin inhibits vitamin K epoxide reductase (factors II, VII, IX, X, proteins C/S).
Platelets: adhere via vWF (to exposed collagen), activate (shape change, granule release — ADP, TXA2), aggregate via GPIIb/IIIa-fibrinogen. vWF also carries factor VIII.
