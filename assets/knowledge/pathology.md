# Pathology

## Cell Injury, Death, and Adaptation
Reversible injury: cellular swelling (hydropic change), fatty change, membrane blebbing, ribosomal detachment. Irreversible: membrane damage, mitochondrial dysfunction (↓ATP, ↑Ca), nuclear changes.
Necrosis nuclear changes: pyknosis (shrinkage) → karyorrhexis (fragmentation) → karyolysis (dissolution).
Necrosis types: coagulative (most organs, ischemia — architecture preserved), liquefactive (brain infarcts, abscesses), caseous (TB — cheese-like), fat (pancreatitis, breast trauma — saponification), fibrinoid (immune vascular damage, malignant HTN), gangrenous (limbs; wet = superimposed infection).
Apoptosis: programmed cell death, energy-dependent, no inflammation. Intrinsic (mitochondrial, Bax/Bak release cytochrome c → caspase 9) vs extrinsic (death receptors Fas/TNF → caspase 8). Bcl-2 anti-apoptotic (overexpressed in follicular lymphoma t(14;18)).
Adaptations: hypertrophy (↑cell size — cardiac in HTN), hyperplasia (↑cell number — endometrial, BPH), atrophy (↓size — disuse, denervation), metaplasia (cell type switch — Barrett esophagus: squamous → columnar; smokers: respiratory → squamous), dysplasia (disordered growth, pre-neoplastic).
Calcification: dystrophic (damaged tissue, normal Ca — atherosclerosis, caseous TB), metastatic (↑Ca — hyperparathyroidism, bone destruction, vitamin D excess, renal failure).

## Inflammation and Repair
Acute inflammation: vascular changes (vasodilation → erythema/warmth; ↑permeability → edema) + cellular events (margination, rolling via selectins, adhesion via integrins, transmigration, chemotaxis). Neutrophils first (6–24 h), monocytes/macrophages later (24–48 h).
Mediators: histamine (mast cells — vasodilation/permeability), prostaglandins (PGE2 pain/fever — COX-2), leukotrienes (LTB4 chemotaxis; LTC4/D4/E4 bronchospasm/permeability), C3a/C5a (anaphylatoxins), C5b-9 (MAC), IL-1/TNF (fever, endothelial activation), IL-8 (neutrophil chemotaxis), IL-6 (acute phase reactants), IFN-γ (macrophage activation), NO.
Chronic inflammation: macrophages, lymphocytes, plasma cells; granulomatous = epithelioid macrophages + giant cells (TB, sarcoidosis, leprosy, cat-scratch, Crohn). Caseating granulomas = TB until proven otherwise.
Wound healing: primary intention (clean, sutured) vs secondary (large, granulation, contraction via myofibroblasts). Phases: hemostasis → inflammation → proliferation (granulation tissue, angiogenesis via VEGF, fibroblasts) → remodeling (collagen type III → I, MMPs).
Granuloma components: central necrosis (caseous in TB), epithelioid macrophages, Langhans giant cells, lymphocyte rim.

## Neoplasia
Benign vs malignant: benign — encapsulated, well-differentiated, slow, no metastasis (-oma). Malignant — invasive, pleomorphic, mitoses, metastasis (carcinoma/sarcoma). Carcinoma in situ = full-thickness dysplasia, basement membrane intact.
Carcinoma = epithelial; sarcoma = mesenchymal; leukemia/lymphoma = hematopoietic; blastoma = embryonal.
Metastasis routes: lymphatic (carcinomas — sentinel node), hematogenous (sarcomas → lung/liver; renal cell → IVC; choriocarcinoma → lung), transcoelomic (ovarian → peritoneum).
Tumor suppressors: p53 (Li-Fraumeni; "guardian of genome", G1 arrest/apoptosis), Rb (retinoblastoma, E2F control), APC (FAP → colorectal cancer; β-catenin/Wnt), BRCA1/2 (breast/ovarian, DNA repair), PTEN (endometrial, glioblastoma; PI3K/AKT), VHL (renal cell carcinoma, hemangioblastoma), NF1 (neurofibromas), WT1 (Wilms tumor).
Oncogenes: RAS (most common — pancreatic, colon, lung), MYC (Burkitt t(8;14)), HER2/ERBB2 (breast), BCR-ABL (CML t(9;22) Philadelphia), RET (MEN2, medullary thyroid), N-MYC (neuroblastoma), c-KIT (GIST).
Hallmarks: self-sufficiency in growth signals, insensitivity to anti-growth, evasion of apoptosis, limitless replication (telomerase), angiogenesis, invasion/metastasis, immune evasion, metabolic reprogramming (Warburg effect).
Cachexia: TNF-α, IL-1, IL-6 mediated wasting. Paraneoplastic syndromes: hypercalcemia (PTHrP — squamous lung), SIADH (small cell lung), Cushing (ACTH — small cell), Trousseau (migratory thrombophlebitis — pancreatic), acanthosis nigricans (GI), Lambert-Eaton (small cell — VGCC antibodies, improves with use), myasthenia (thymoma), dermatomyositis (ovarian/lung).
Grading (differentiation) vs staging (spread — TNM; more prognostic).

## Hematopathology
Anemias by MCV:
- Microcytic (↓MCV): iron deficiency (↓ferritin, ↑TIBC, ↑RDW; koilonychia, pica, Plummer-Vinson), thalassemia (normal/↑iron, target cells; α = gene deletion, β = point mutation; β-thal major = Cooley anemia, crew-cut skull, chipmunk facies), anemia of chronic disease (↑ferritin, ↓TIBC, ↑hepcidin), sideroblastic (ringed sideroblasts; lead poisoning — basophilic stippling), lead poisoning.
- Normocytic: acute blood loss, hemolysis, renal failure (↓EPO), aplastic anemia, early iron/B12 deficiency.
- Macrocytic (↑MCV): megaloblastic (B12 — neurologic symptoms, ↑methylmalonic acid, ↑homocysteine; folate — no neuro, ↑homocysteine only; hypersegmented neutrophils) vs non-megaloblastic (alcohol, liver disease, hypothyroid, MDS, orotic aciduria).
Hemolytic anemias: intrinsic — hereditary spherocytosis (spectrin/ankyrin; osmotic fragility, splenectomy curative), G6PD deficiency (oxidative stress → Heinz bodies, bite cells; X-linked), sickle cell (HbS, Glu→Val β6; sickling with hypoxia/dehydration; autosplenectomy → encapsulated organisms; dactylitis, aplastic crisis with parvovirus B19, acute chest syndrome), PNH (CD55/CD59 deficiency; complement-mediated hemolysis, thrombosis, pancytopenia). Extrinsic — autoimmune (warm IgG — SLE, CLL, methyldopa; cold IgM — Mycoplasma, EBV), microangiopathic (TTP — ADAMTS13 deficiency, pentad; HUS — Shiga toxin, renal failure; DIC), mechanical valves.
Polycythemia: vera (JAK2 V617F, ↓EPO, pruritus after shower, erythromelalgia, thrombosis) vs secondary (↑EPO — hypoxia, RCC, HCC).
Leukemias:
- ALL: children, TdT+, B-cell common (CD10); Down syndrome; testicular/CNS sanctuary sites.
- AML: adults, Auer rods, MPO+, t(15;17) APL (DIC — treat with ATRA).
- CLL: elderly, smudge cells, CD5+/CD20+, autoimmune hemolysis, Richter transformation → DLBCL.
- CML: t(9;22) BCR-ABL, ↑basophils, splenomegaly, low LAP; blast crisis; imatinib.
Lymphomas: Hodgkin — Reed-Sternberg cells (CD15+/CD30+, owl-eye), contiguous spread, bimodal age, B symptoms, better prognosis. Non-Hodgkin — multiple sites, extranodal. Burkitt t(8;14) c-MYC, starry-sky, EBV, jaw (endemic)/abdomen (sporadic). Follicular t(14;18) BCL-2, painless waxing/waning. Mantle cell t(11;14) cyclin D1. Marginal zone (MALT — H. pylori). DLBCL most common NHL. Mycosis fungoides/Sézary (cutaneous T-cell).
Multiple myeloma: plasma cell neoplasm, CRAB (Calcium ↑, Renal failure, Anemia, Bone lytic lesions), M-spike (IgG/IgA), Bence-Jones protein, rouleaux, amyloidosis. MGUS precursor. Waldenström = IgM hyperviscosity, no bone lesions.

## Cardiovascular Pathology
Atherosclerosis: endothelial injury → LDL oxidation → foam cells → fatty streak → fibrous plaque → complicated lesion. Risk: HTN, smoking, diabetes, hyperlipidemia. Locations: abdominal aorta > coronary > popliteal.
MI: transmural (LAD most common), timeline — 0–4 h no changes; 4–24 h coagulative necrosis, wavy fibers; 1–3 d neutrophils; 3–7 d macrophages (rupture risk — free wall → tamponade, septum → VSD, papillary muscle → MR); 1–3 w granulation; >3 w scar. Complications: arrhythmia (most common death), HF, pericarditis (Dressler = autoimmune, weeks later), aneurysm, mural thrombus.
Biomarkers: troponin I/T (most specific, rises 2–4 h, stays 7–10 d), CK-MB (reinfarction detection).
Hypertension: essential (95%); malignant = fibrinoid necrosis, hyperplastic arteriolosclerosis (onion-skinning), papilledema. LVH, nephrosclerosis, retinopathy.
Heart failure: left (dyspnea, orthopnea, pulmonary edema — heart failure cells, Kerley B lines), right (JVD, hepatomegaly, peripheral edema, nutmeg liver, cardiac cirrhosis). Cor pulmonale = right HF from lung disease. Dilated cardiomyopathy (most common; alcohol, Coxsackie B, doxorubicin, Chagas, peripartum), hypertrophic (sarcomere mutations, β-myosin heavy chain; asymmetric septal hypertrophy, sudden death in athletes; systolic murmur ↑ with Valsalva), restrictive (amyloid, sarcoid, hemochromatosis, endomyocardial fibrosis).
Valvular: rheumatic fever (GAS → molecular mimicry; mitral stenosis most common; fish-mouth valve, Aschoff bodies, Anitschkow cells). Aortic stenosis (calcific elderly, bicuspid younger; crescendo-decrescendo, SAD: syncope/angina/dyspnea). Mitral regurgitation (MVP most common in developed world; rheumatic worldwide). Aortic regurgitation (bicuspid, syphilis, Marfan; water-hammer pulse, wide pulse pressure).
Endocarditis: acute (S. aureus, IVDU tricuspid), subacute (viridans strep on damaged valves). Duke criteria. Culture-negative: Coxiella, Bartonella, HACEK. Nonbacterial thrombotic (marantic — malignancy), Libman-Sacks (SLE).
Pericarditis: friction rub, diffuse ST elevation, PR depression; causes — viral, uremia, post-MI, SLE, TB.
Aortic dissection: intimal tear, HTN, Marfan/cystic medial necrosis; tearing chest pain radiating to back; type A (ascending — surgery), type B (descending). Aortic aneurysm: abdominal (atherosclerosis, infrarenal), thoracic (HTN, syphilis — vasa vasorum, Marfan).

## Pulmonary Pathology
COPD: chronic bronchitis (productive cough 3 months × 2 years; mucus gland hyperplasia, Reid index ↑; blue bloater) + emphysema (alveolar wall destruction; centriacinar — smoking, upper lobes; panacinar — α1-antitrypsin, lower lobes; pink puffer). ↑RV, ↑FRC, ↓FEV1/FVC.
Asthma: reversible airway obstruction, eosinophils, Charcot-Leyden crystals, Curschmann spirals, mucus plugging.
Bronchiectasis: permanent bronchial dilation; CF, Kartagener (situs inversus), post-infectious; copious purulent sputum.
Restrictive: interstitial lung diseases — idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (usual interstitial pneumonia, honeycombing), sarcoidosis (noncaseating granulomas, ↑ACE, bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy, erythema nodosum, hypercalcemia), hypersensitivity pneumonitis, pneumoconioses (coal, silicosis — upper lobes, eggshell calcification; asbestosis — lower lobes, ferruginous bodies, mesothelioma).
Lung cancer: small cell (central, neuroendocrine, paraneoplastic — ACTH/SIADH/Lambert-Eaton, chemo/radiation only), squamous (central, smoking, keratin pearls, intercellular bridges, PTHrP, cavitation), adenocarcinoma (most common overall and in nonsmokers, peripheral, glands/mucin), large cell (peripheral, anaplastic, gynecomastia), carcinoid (neuroendocrine, good prognosis). Pancoast (apex — Horner, brachial plexus).
Pleural: effusion transudate (HF, cirrhosis, nephrotic) vs exudate (infection, malignancy; Light criteria). Empyema, hemothorax, chylothorax (thoracic duct). Pneumothorax: spontaneous (tall thin males, apical blebs), tension (mediastinal shift, tracheal deviation — needle decompression 2nd ICS MCL).
Pneumonia: lobar (S. pneumoniae; stages: congestion → red hepatization → gray hepatization → resolution), bronchopneumonia (patchy), interstitial/atypical (Mycoplasma, viruses, Legionella, Chlamydia, Coxiella). Aspiration — right lower lobe.

## GI and Hepatobiliary Pathology
Esophagus: GERD → Barrett (intestinal metaplasia with goblet cells → adenocarcinoma risk). Squamous cell (upper/mid, smoking/alcohol, hot liquids) vs adenocarcinoma (lower, Barrett). Mallory-Weiss (mucosal tear, hematemesis after vomiting) vs Boerhaave (transmural rupture). Zenker diverticulum (posterior hypopharynx, halitosis). Achalasia (loss of myenteric plexus; bird-beak; Chagas).
Stomach: H. pylori (antrum → duodenal ulcer; body → gastric ulcer, MALT lymphoma, adenocarcinoma). Peptic ulcer: duodenal (pain improves with food, posterior wall → gastroduodenal artery bleed). Gastric cancer: intestinal type (H. pylori, nitrosamines, blood type A) vs diffuse (linitis plastica, signet ring cells, E-cadherin/CDH1). Virchow node (left supraclavicular), Sister Mary Joseph nodule, Krukenberg tumor (ovary).
IBD: Crohn (any GI, transmural, skip lesions, cobblestoning, noncaseating granulomas, creeping fat, fistulas/strictures; ASCA+, NOD2) vs ulcerative colitis (colon only, continuous from rectum, mucosal/submucosal, pseudopolyps, crypt abscesses; p-ANCA; toxic megacolon, primary sclerosing cholangitis). Both ↑ colon cancer risk.
Appendicitis: obstruction (fecalith) → inflammation; periumbilical → RLQ pain (McBurney).
Liver: fatty change (alcohol, metabolic syndrome), alcoholic hepatitis (Mallory-Denk bodies, neutrophils), cirrhosis (regenerative nodules + fibrosis; portal HTN → varices, splenomegaly, ascites, caput medusae). Viral hepatitis: HBV (ground-glass hepatocytes, HBsAg carrier → HCC; serology: HBsAg = infection, anti-HBs = immunity, anti-HBc IgM = acute, HBeAg = high infectivity), HCV (chronic → cirrhosis → HCC; most common cause of chronic hepatitis). Hemochromatosis (HFE, bronze diabetes, HCC), Wilson (ATP7B, copper, Kayser-Fleischer rings, neuropsychiatric), α1-antitrypsin (PAS+ globules, cirrhosis + emphysema). Budd-Chiari (hepatic vein thrombosis). Reye syndrome (aspirin in kids — mitochondrial damage).
Gallbladder: cholesterol stones (4 F's: female, fat, forty, fertile), pigment stones (hemolysis, cirrhosis). Cholecystitis (cystic duct obstruction; Murphy sign), cholangitis (Charcot triad: fever, RUQ pain, jaundice; Reynolds pentad + hypotension/confusion). Porcelain gallbladder → cancer risk.
Pancreas: acute pancreatitis (gallstones, alcohol; epigastric → back; ↑lipase/amylase; Grey Turner/Cullen signs; Ranson criteria), chronic (calcifications, steatorrhea, diabetes; alcohol), adenocarcinoma (head — painless jaundice, Courvoisier sign, Trousseau; CA 19-9).
Colon: polyps — adenomatous (tubular, tubulovillous, villous — malignant potential; APC → KRAS → p53 sequence), hyperplastic (benign), serrated. FAP (APC, 100s polyps, prophylactic colectomy), Lynch/HNPCC (mismatch repair, right-sided, endometrial cancer). Colorectal cancer: iron deficiency anemia (right), obstruction/hematochezia (left), CEA marker.

## Renal Pathology
Nephritic: hematuria, RBC casts, HTN, oliguria, mild proteinuria. Nephrotic: >3.5 g/d proteinuria, hypoalbuminemia, edema, hyperlipidemia, lipiduria (fatty casts, oval fat bodies).
Nephritic syndromes: post-streptococcal GN (children, 2–4 weeks after pharyngitis/skin infection, subepithelial humps, ↓C3, lumpy-bumpy IF), IgA nephropathy/Berger (most common worldwide; hematuria concurrent with URI/GI infection), rapidly progressive/crescentic GN (crescents = fibrin + macrophages; Goodpasture — linear IF anti-GBM, lung hemorrhage; granulomatosis with polyangiitis — c-ANCA; lupus nephritis), Alport (type IV collagen, X-linked; hematuria + deafness + ocular).
Nephrotic syndromes: minimal change disease (children, effacement of foot processes, steroid-responsive, selective proteinuria), focal segmental glomerulosclerosis (most common in adults, African Americans, HIV/heroin/sickle), membranous nephropathy (most common in white adults; subepithelial spikes, PLA2R antibodies; HBV, SLE, drugs, solid tumors), membranoproliferative (tram-track, HBV/HCV, cryoglobulinemia), diabetic nephropathy (Kimmelstiel-Wilson nodules; most common cause of nephrotic syndrome overall), amyloidosis (Congo red, apple-green birefringence).
Tubulointerstitial: acute tubular necrosis (most common cause of AKI in hospital; ischemic or nephrotoxic — aminoglycosides, contrast, myoglobin; muddy brown casts), acute interstitial nephritis (drugs — penicillins, NSAIDs, PPIs; fever, rash, eosinophilia), pyelonephritis (ascending UTI; WBC casts; xanthogranulomatous with Proteus staghorn), chronic pyelonephritis (reflux/obstruction; thyroidization).
Kidney stones: calcium oxalate (most common; envelope/dumbbell), struvite (magnesium ammonium phosphate; staghorn; urease+ organisms — Proteus), uric acid (radiolucent; gout, leukemia), cystine (hexagonal; cystinuria).
Renal tumors: renal cell carcinoma (clear cell most common; VHL; hematuria, flank pain, mass; paraneoplastic — EPO, PTHrP, ACTH; varicocele left side), Wilms tumor (children, WT1, triphasic histology, WAGR/Denys-Drash/Beckwith-Wiedemann), transitional cell carcinoma (renal pelvis/bladder; smoking, aniline dyes, cyclophosphamide — painless hematuria).
Bladder: urothelial carcinoma (painless hematuria; schistosomiasis → squamous cell).

## Breast and Female Genital Pathology
Breast: fibroadenoma (most common benign, mobile), phyllodes (leaf-like, stromal), fibrocystic change (cysts, apocrine metaplasia), intraductal papilloma (bloody nipple discharge), fat necrosis (post-trauma, calcified). Carcinoma: invasive ductal (most common, scirrhous), invasive lobular (bilateral, single-file cells, E-cadherin loss), DCIS (comedo necrosis, calcifications), Paget disease (nipple eczema, underlying DCIS), inflammatory (dermal lymphatic invasion, peau d'orange, worst prognosis). BRCA1 (triple-negative), BRCA2. HER2 amplification (trastuzumab), ER/PR+ (tamoxifen, aromatase inhibitors).
Cervix: HPV 16/18 → CIN → squamous cell carcinoma (transformation zone); screening Pap + HPV testing. Koilocytes.
Endometrium: hyperplasia (unopposed estrogen → adenocarcinoma; type I endometrioid — PTEN, obesity, tamoxifen, Lynch), leiomyoma/fibroid (most common uterine tumor, estrogen-sensitive), leiomyosarcoma (de novo).
Ovary: surface epithelial (serous cystadenocarcinoma most common malignant; BRCA, CA-125), germ cell (teratoma — mature benign, immature malignant, struma ovarii; dysgerminoma = female seminoma, LDH/hCG; yolk sac = Schiller-Duval bodies, AFP), sex cord-stromal (granulosa — estrogen, Call-Exner bodies, precocious puberty/endometrial hyperplasia; fibroma — Meigs syndrome; Sertoli-Leydig — androgens). Metastatic: Krukenberg (gastric signet ring).
PCOS: anovulation, hyperandrogenism, polycystic ovaries; ↑LH/FSH ratio, insulin resistance, endometrial cancer risk.
Gestational: hydatidiform mole (complete — 46XX all paternal, snowstorm, ↑hCG, no fetus; partial — triploid, some fetal tissue), choriocarcinoma (malignant trophoblast, hematogenous lung mets, ↑hCG, no villi), placenta accreta/increta/percreta (no decidua basalis; prior C-section).

## Endocrine Pathology
Thyroid: papillary carcinoma (most common; Orphan Annie eyes, psammoma bodies, nuclear grooves; lymphatic spread, radiation exposure, excellent prognosis), follicular (hematogenous spread; invasion distinguishes from adenoma), medullary (C cells, calcitonin, amyloid stroma; MEN2A/2B RET), anaplastic (elderly, worst). Hashimoto (anti-TPO, anti-thyroglobulin; Hürthle cells; ↑lymphoma risk), Graves (TSI → diffuse goiter, exophthalmos, pretibial myxedema), subacute/De Quervain (painful, post-viral, granulomatous), Riedel (fibrosis, IgG4).
Parathyroid: primary hyperparathyroidism (adenoma most common; stones, bones, groans, psychiatric overtones), secondary (renal failure — ↓Ca, ↑PO4), tertiary (autonomous after long secondary).
Adrenal: Cushing syndrome (exogenous steroids most common; pituitary adenoma = Cushing disease; ectopic ACTH — small cell; adrenal tumor; dexamethasone suppression, 24-h urine cortisol), Addison (autoimmune most common in developed world, TB worldwide; hyperpigmentation, hyperkalemia, hyponatremia), Conn (aldosterone adenoma — HTN, hypoK, metabolic alkalosis), pheochromocytoma (rule of 10s; catecholamines; episodic HTN, headache, sweating; diagnose with metanephrines; α block before β block), neuroblastoma (children, adrenal medulla; N-MYC, Homer-Wright rosettes, opsoclonus-myoclonus, crosses midline vs Wilms doesn't).
Pituitary: prolactinoma (most common; amenorrhea/galactorrhea, hypogonadism; treat with dopamine agonists), GH adenoma (gigantism/acromegaly; IGF-1), ACTH (Cushing disease), nonfunctioning (mass effect, bitemporal hemianopia), Sheehan (postpartum pituitary necrosis), apoplexy (hemorrhage into adenoma), empty sella, craniopharyngioma (Rathke pouch, calcifications, children).
MEN syndromes: MEN1 (3 P's: Parathyroid, Pituitary, Pancreatic; menin gene), MEN2A (medullary thyroid, pheochromocytoma, parathyroid; RET), MEN2B (medullary thyroid, pheo, mucosal neuromas, marfanoid; RET).
Diabetes pathology: type 1 (autoimmune β-cell destruction, HLA-DR3/DR4, insulitis, ketosis-prone), type 2 (insulin resistance → amyloid deposition in islets). Complications: nonenzymatic glycosylation (atherosclerosis, microangiopathy), osmotic damage (sorbitol — cataracts, neuropathy). Nephropathy (GBM thickening → Kimmelstiel-Wilson), retinopathy (nonproliferative: microaneurysms, dot-blot; proliferative: neovascularization), neuropathy (glove-stocking, autonomic).

## Musculoskeletal and Skin Pathology
Bone tumors: osteoma (benign, Gardner syndrome), osteoid osteoma (night pain relieved by aspirin, nidus), osteoblastoma (spine, not aspirin-responsive), giant cell tumor (epiphysis, soap-bubble, 20–40), osteosarcoma (most common primary malignant in young; metaphysis, sunburst, Codman triangle, Rb/p53, Paget/radiation in elderly), Ewing sarcoma (diaphysis, onion-skin, t(11;22), CD99, boys <15), chondrosarcoma (elderly, pelvis). Metastases to bone most common overall (breast, prostate, lung, kidney, thyroid — "Lead Kettle"). Prostate = blastic; others mostly lytic.
Arthritis: osteoarthritis (wear-and-tear, DIP/PIP/weight-bearing, osteophytes, Heberden/Bouchard nodes, ↓with rest), rheumatoid (autoimmune, MCP/PIP symmetric, spares DIP, pannus, rheumatoid factor + anti-CCP, morning stiffness >1 h, extra-articular: nodules, vasculitis, Felty), gout (monosodium urate, needle-shaped negative birefringence, 1st MTP podagra, tophi), pseudogout (CPPD, rhomboid positive birefringence, knee, chondrocalcinosis), septic arthritis (S. aureus; gonococcal in young sexually active), ankylosing spondylitis (HLA-B27, bamboo spine, sacroiliitis, uveitis, aortitis), reactive arthritis (post-GI/GU infection; can't see, can't pee, can't climb a tree), psoriatic (dactylitis, pencil-in-cup).
Vasculitis: large vessel — giant cell/temporal arteritis (elderly, jaw claudication, visual loss, ↑ESR, assoc. polymyalgia rheumatica; steroids before biopsy), Takayasu (young Asian women, pulseless disease). Medium — polyarteritis nodosa (HBV, renal/visceral, spares lungs, microaneurysms, livedo), Kawasaki (children, CRASH and burn: Conjunctivitis, Rash, Adenopathy, Strawberry tongue, Hand/foot edema + fever; coronary aneurysms). Small — GPA (c-ANCA, PR3; upper/lower airway + kidney), EGPA/Churg-Strauss (p-ANCA, asthma, eosinophilia), microscopic polyangiitis (p-ANCA, MPO; lung-kidney, no granulomas), IgA vasculitis/Henoch-Schönlein (children, palpable purpura, arthritis, abdominal pain, IgA nephropathy), cryoglobulinemia (HCV).
Skin: melanoma (ABCD; Breslow depth most prognostic; S-100), basal cell carcinoma (most common skin cancer, pearly rolled border, telangiectasia, sun-exposed), squamous cell (keratoacanthoma variant, Marjolin ulcer in burns), actinic keratosis (premalignant → SCC), seborrheic keratosis (stuck-on, Leser-Trélat sign = sudden eruption → GI malignancy), pemphigus vulgaris (intraepidermal, anti-desmoglein, flaccid bullae, +Nikolsky, reticular IF), bullous pemphigoid (subepidermal, anti-hemidesmosome, tense bullae, -Nikolsky, linear IF), dermatitis herpetiformis (celiac, IgA at dermal papillae), psoriasis (Auspitz sign, Munro microabscesses, nail pitting), lichen planus (6 P's: purple, pruritic, polygonal, planar, papules, plaques; Wickham striae; hepatitis C), erythema multiforme (target lesions, HSV/Mycoplasma/drugs; SJS/TEN severe spectrum), lupus (malar rash, discoid, photosensitivity, interface dermatitis).
